On gcc/x86 this is 8 bytes: a and b share the first 4-byte unit (3+6 = 9 bits used), the anonymous zero-width
field forces c to start a new unit, so c occupies a second 4-byte unit. What is implementation-defined: whether
allocation starts from the LSB or the MSB end, whether fields may straddle unit boundaries, and the padding
rules. That is why register maps use explicit masks and shifts instead — the same struct can map field a to
bits [2:0] on one toolchain and [31:29] on another.
